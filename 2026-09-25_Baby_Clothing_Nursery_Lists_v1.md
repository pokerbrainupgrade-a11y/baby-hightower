## Buying rules (apply to both lists)

1. **Skin-contact fabric:** GOTS-certified organic cotton first; merino wool second; cotton muslin for blankets and swaddles. GOTS is the verification — "organic" on a tag alone isn't.
2. **Treat these with caution:** polyester fleece and microfiber against skin (sheds microfibers, traps heat), bamboo "viscose/rayon" (chemically regenerated; soft, but not the natural fiber the marketing implies).
3. **Sleepwear:** US rules require kids' sleepwear to be either flame-resistant or snug-fitting. Choose **snug-fit cotton** (tag reads "wear snug-fitting, not flame resistant") — that's the untreated route.
4. **Fragrance-free everything** — detergent, wipes, balms. No essential oils on baby in the early months.
5. **Heat is the design constraint.** Baby is 0–3 months in May–Aug and 3–6 months in Aug–Nov. Short sleeves carry the load; a few long-sleeve pieces cover AC.
6. **Size up bottoms for cloth diapers.** Cloth adds bulk in the seat. Bodysuits and pants run tight a size early; bodysuit extenders are a cheap fix.
7. **Buy thin on Newborn size.** Average birth weight is ~7.5 lb; NB fits roughly 5–8 lb and many babies outgrow it in 2–4 weeks or skip it. Bulk up on 0–3. Hold most 3–6 purchases until after the shower and the first growth check — gifts pile up in exactly these sizes.
8. **Quantities assume laundry every 2–3 days** (cloth diapers set that rhythm) and 2–4 outfit changes a day for spit-up and blowouts.

Priority key: **Must** = have before birth · **Nice** = useful, not required · **Later** = buy when the stage arrives · **Skip** = commonly sold, not needed

**Material honesty:** Most covers are PUL (polyester laminated with polyurethane). It doesn't touch skin — the cotton does — but wool covers are the only fully natural option.

**Prep timing:** Natural fibers must be washed 3–5+ times hot to reach full absorbency. Prep the whole stash by **week 36 (~mid-April 2027)**.

**Rough cost:** ~$500–800 all-in for this system, versus roughly $2,000–3,000 for disposables through potty training. Estimate only.

### 2D. Skin care & lotion

**Mechanism first:** Newborn skin rarely needs lotion. The peeling in the first 2 weeks is normal shedding, not dryness. Two large trials of daily emollients from birth (BEEP, PreventADALL) found no eczema prevention, and BEEP saw slightly more skin infections. So: **spot-treat dry patches only.** Olive oil specifically weakens the skin barrier in infant studies — avoid it. Sunflower seed oil has the better barrier profile (evidence modest).

| Item | Qty | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homemade balm (recipe below) **or** a fragrance-free natural balm | 1 jar | Nice | Balm, not lotion. |
| Sunflower seed oil (cold-pressed) | 1 | Nice | Massage and spot dryness. |

**Homemade balm — why a balm, not a lotion:** anything with water in it grows bacteria and mold without a preservative. A water-free balm doesn't.

- 2 parts shea butter (unrefined) · 2 parts sunflower seed oil · 1 part beeswax pastilles, by weight
- Melt in a double boiler, pour into sterilized glass jars, cool, lid.
- Small batches; use within ~3 months. No essential oils. Patch-test on a small spot first.
- Grass-fed tallow balm is a common alternative — evidence is anecdotal, same patch-test rule.
- Balms and oils cause cloth-diaper repelling — use a paper liner when applied to the diaper area.

## Decisions to make

1. **Cloth from day one, or a 1–2 week disposable bridge?** Meconium (the first tar-like stools) and the cord stump push many cloth families to unscented, chlorine-free disposables for the first couple of weeks. Ask Wombkeepers what they use at the Renewal Center.
2. **Prefolds + covers (economical, simplest) vs. all-in-ones (fastest, pricier, harder to dry).** List above assumes prefolds + covers.
3. **Registry vs. buy:** clothing and cloth diapers are ideal Illinois-shower registry items (Mar 13).
